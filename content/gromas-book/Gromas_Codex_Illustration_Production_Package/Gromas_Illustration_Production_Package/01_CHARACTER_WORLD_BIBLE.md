# Gromas and the Gobbledygooks
## Character, World, and Visual Continuity Bible

## Visual thesis

The book should feel like a carefully observed miniature world discovered inside an ordinary apartment. The Gobbledygooks are funny and fantastical, but their labor, worry, courage, and relief should feel emotionally real. The illustration language combines hand-painted watercolor and gouache, fine graphite-like edges, subtle colored-pencil texture, atmospheric light, and cinematic scale. Quiet scenes may dissolve into warm paper through vignetted edges; action and cutaway scenes may run full bleed.

The supplied Gromas image establishes the character identity, not the final rendering medium. Preserve his design while translating the glossy reference into a tactile literary picture-book surface.

## Naming glossary

- **Gromas** — main character; do not spell “Groans,” “Gromus,” or “Gromas Bretzgard.”
- **Gobbledygook** — one member of the species.
- **Gobbledygooks** — plural and the name of the community.
- **Chet** — second-in-command and inventor.
- **Spinette / Spinettes** — the pressure-generating device.
- **Great Power Supply** — the central power apparatus.
- **Grid** — the connected network.
- **Bobby** — the couple’s dog.

## Gromas — locked

Use `references/gromas_reference.jpeg` as the source of truth.

- Approximately 4.5 inches tall in-world.
- Moss-green body made of long rounded ribbon-like organic strands.
- Deep teal shadows and sage/lichen highlights.
- Large round cream eyes with warm brown irises and dark pupils.
- Tiny black mouth; no visible nose.
- Rounded limbs, four-digit hands, broad three-lobed feet.
- Soft tactile surface; never slimy, furry, or made of human hair.
- Kind, earnest, capable, encouraging, and occasionally very tired.
- Optional wooden broom-like staff/pointer from the source image; it is not mandatory in every scene.

## Chet — provisional until reference arrives

- Same species, clearly related to Gromas.
- Slightly shorter and stockier.
- Blue-green body; upward-swept crown strands.
- Copper goggles on forehead; narrow utility belt.
- Inventive, quick, warm, and energetic.
- Presents the Spinette and, under the production interpretation, calls “Spindection” and assigns teams.

## Supporting Gobbledygooks

Vary height, width, strand length, eye shape, and controlled green/olive/teal hue. Keep shared anatomy consistent. Accessories identify roles without turning the community into a military force.

### Frontliners

Sturdy builders and installers. Small hammers, hand saws, clamps, work belts, and Spinette frames.

### Electricians

Copper wire coils, meters, ceramic insulators, small goggles, and careful hand tools.

### Lookouts

Listening horns, binoculars, flags, ladders, blankets, and compact forts. They maintain the toast stockpile.

## Humans

The man and woman are ordinary, warm, contemporary apartment residents. They are unaware of the Gobbledygooks and are not villains. Keep them mostly cropped—hands, shoes, legs, partial torsos—so the miniature scale remains primary. The man’s black shoe is a locked continuity object for pages 24–27.

## Bobby

A friendly medium-sized tan or golden dog with a plain collar. Bobby appears during the morning routine and should never threaten the Gobbledygooks.

## The Spinette

A shallow, quiet under-floor pressure generator. Its visual logic must stay identical:

1. Human foot presses floorboard.
2. Compression plate moves.
3. Spiral cam or rack turns the copper coil and small flywheel.
4. Contained charge enters two copper leads.
5. Grid carries power to the Great Power Supply.

Never depict it as a mine, trap, weapon, exposed saw, or dangerous shock device.

## The Great Power Supply

Handmade brass/copper/glass/wood apparatus with a visible vertical bar. Maintain three states:

- **Low:** faint amber ember, almost-empty bar.
- **Empty:** dark glass, dead lamps.
- **Charging/restored:** warm golden fill rising clearly.

## World scale and materials

The community occupies a 10- to 12-inch floor-joist cavity, making Gromas’s 4.5-inch height plausible and a human shoe enormous. Reuse wood scraps, copper, brass, glass, ceramic, fabric, string, thimble-like containers, and tiny tools. Avoid random modern screens, oversized human electronics, or generic fantasy castles.

## Color and lighting arc

1. **Spreads 1–4:** moss, teal, weak amber; worry turning into invention.
2. **Spreads 5–7:** workshop gold, copper, and warm toast; preparation and labor.
3. **Spreads 8–9:** sunrise orange and depleted ember; urgency, then blackout blue.
4. **Spreads 10–11:** navy below, cream morning above; suspense.
5. **Spread 12:** luminous butter-gold and copper; payoff.
6. **Spread 13:** rich amber softening to violet; celebration and fatigue.
7. **Spread 14:** bedside amber fading to deep navy; peace.

## Recurring visual jokes and motifs

- Warm buttered toast as serious field provisions.
- Tiny forts built with great dignity.
- Large human objects treated as architecture.
- Chet’s goggles and tools.
- The Great Power Supply bar quietly tracking the emotional stakes.

## Continuity priorities

1. Gromas’s face and silhouette.
2. Spinette construction.
3. Great Power Supply design and state.
4. Human black shoe.
5. Chet’s color, crown strands, goggles, and belt.
6. Scale relationship between Gobbledygooks, joists, and humans.
7. Time-of-day and lighting arc.
